How to read this chart

Each point is one weekly close — the price at 00:00 UTC on Monday, when one trading week rolls into the next. Weekly closes strip out the intraday noise that dominates shorter timeframes: on a one-minute chart bitcoin looks chaotic; on a weekly chart, 2026 resolves into three clean acts.

Act one, January–February: the flush. The year opened near $101,000 and slid for five straight weeks, bottoming at $95,200 in the first week of February — the only sustained dip below six figures all year. Sellers were largely long-term holders distributing into thin winter liquidity, a pattern bitcoin has repeated in the January of most post-halving years.

Act two, March–July: the grind up. Sixteen weeks of higher lows carried price from under $100K to the mid-$120Ks. Persistent spot ETF inflows did most of the lifting: US-listed funds absorbed more coins than miners produced in 21 of those 24 weeks, and the bid simply never left.

Act three, August–September: the peak and the pause. The year-to-date high printed at $129,800 in the first week of August, just above the October 2025 all-time high near $126,000. Price has since consolidated between $112,000 and $120,000 — a 7% band that, by bitcoin's historical standards, counts as quiet.

Levels traders are watching

  • $129,800 — the 2026 high and immediate overhead resistance. A weekly close above it opens price discovery.
  • $110,000 — the September reaction low and the midpoint of the autumn consolidation.
  • $95,200 — the February low and the year's line in the sand; it held on the first test and was never revisited.
  • $100,000 — purely psychological, but round six-figure numbers have acted as magnets since December 2024.
