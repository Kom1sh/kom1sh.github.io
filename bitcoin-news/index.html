Spot ETF inflows hit $4.1B in September, the strongest month since January

US-listed spot bitcoin funds closed September with net inflows of roughly $4.1 billion, the best monthly print since the start of the year. IBIT accounted for just over half the total. The bid matters mechanically: at current issuance of ~450 BTC per day, a $4 billion month absorbs more than twice the coins miners produce. Flow desks describe the buying as steady asset-allocator rebalancing rather than retail momentum chasing.

Hashrate prints a new record above 1.1 ZH/s as miners digest the halving

Bitcoin's seven-day average hashrate crossed 1.1 zettahashes per second for the first time, up about 35% year over year. The milestone is notable because miner revenue per block was cut to 3.125 BTC in April 2024 — operators are deploying more machines for fewer coins, a bet that scale and cheap power beat the subsidy cut. Difficulty adjusted upward 4.2% at the last retarget.

Senate committee advances market-structure bill with explicit bitcoin custody rules

The Senate Banking Committee voted 14–9 to advance a digital-asset market-structure bill that, for the first time, spells out qualified-custody standards for bitcoin held by federally chartered banks. The text treats BTC separately from other tokens, reflecting the CFTC's long-standing classification of bitcoin as a commodity. A floor vote is expected before year-end; the House passed its own version in July.

Corporate treasuries now hold over 1.2 million BTC, led by Strategy's 640,000

Public-company balance sheets crossed 1.2 million BTC this quarter — roughly 6% of total supply. Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) remains the anchor position at approximately 640,000 coins, but the tail is lengthening: more than 140 public companies now disclose bitcoin holdings, up from about 70 at the end of 2024. Auditors have settled into fair-value accounting under the FASB rule that took effect in 2025, removing the old impairment-only distortion.

Bitcoin touches $129,800, a marginal new all-time high, then fades

BTC printed $129,800 in the first week of August, edging past the October 2025 record near $126,000 before sellers emerged. The move had the hallmarks of a stop-run: thin summer books, a fast push through prior highs, and an equally fast rejection. Price has since consolidated between $112,000 and $120,000. The full tape is on our 2026 chart; the cycle context sits in the price history.

Lightning capacity and exchange balances both hit multi-year extremes

Two supply-side trends converged this quarter: coins held on exchanges fell to their lowest share of circulating supply since 2018, while Lightning Network public channel capacity set a new high. Read together, they describe a market where fewer coins sit ready to sell and more sit deployed in payment rails and cold storage — one reason dips in 2026 have been shallower than the drawdowns of prior cycles.
