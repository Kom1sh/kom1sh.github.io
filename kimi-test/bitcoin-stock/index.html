"Bitcoin stock" doesn't exist. Here's what you're actually looking for.

Bitcoin isn't a company, so it has no shares and no stock price. But there are exchange-traded funds that hold bitcoin and trade exactly like stocks — and the distinction matters more than most guides admit.

BTC is a ticker, not a stock

When someone searches for a "bitcoin stock price," they're looking for $114,650 — the spot price of one bitcoin, quoted on exchanges under the ticker BTC (or XBT on some traditional terminals). A stock represents fractional ownership of a business, with a claim on its earnings. Bitcoin has no business behind it: no board, no revenue, no earnings call. Its value rests on scarcity and demand, closer to a commodity like gold than to equity. That difference is a feature to some and a dealbreaker to others — but it means bitcoin can't be analyzed with P/E ratios, and no CEO can dilute it.

The ETFs that made bitcoin stock-shaped

On January 10, 2024, the SEC approved eleven spot bitcoin ETFs, and the practical meaning of "buying bitcoin" changed overnight. Each fund holds actual BTC with a qualified custodian and issues shares that trade on stock exchanges during market hours:

IBIT became the fastest-growing ETF in history, and US spot funds collectively hold over 1.3 million BTC — more than 6% of all coins that will ever exist. For an IRA or a brokerage account, ETFs are the path of least resistance: familiar tax reporting, no wallets, no keys.

ETF shares versus holding actual BTC

The trade-off is real. An ETF share tracks the price but isn't the asset: you can't withdraw it, spend it, or hold it outside market hours — ETFs sleep on weekends while bitcoin trades through them, which is why Monday opens sometimes gap. You also pay the expense ratio every year, forever. Direct ownership gives you the bearer asset itself — sendable anywhere, settleable in minutes, yours without an intermediary — at the cost of custody responsibility. A lost private key is an unrecoverable loss; that risk is why roughly a third of ETF buyers say they chose the wrapper.

How bitcoin behaves next to stocks

Bitcoin's correlation to the Nasdaq has swung between strongly positive (liquidity-driven years like 2020–2022) and near zero. Its long-run return profile dwarfs equities — and so does its drawdown profile: the S&P 500's worst year since 2008 was −37%; bitcoin has lost more than that in a quarter six separate times. Frequently asked questions

Is bitcoin a stock or a currency?

Neither, legally speaking. In the US, the CFTC treats bitcoin as a commodity and the IRS taxes it as property. It trades like a currency pair, is regulated like a commodity, and is taxed like a collectible.

What is the bitcoin stock ticker?

BTC is the asset's ticker. IBIT and FBTC are stock tickers for funds that hold bitcoin. MSTR (Strategy) is a software company whose share price largely tracks its ~640,000 BTC treasury.

Can I buy bitcoin in my 401(k) or IRA?

Directly, usually not — but spot bitcoin ETFs like IBIT are available in most IRA and many 401(k) brokerage windows, which is a large part of why the funds have grown so quickly.
