How bitcoin's market cap is calculated

Market capitalization is borrowed from equity markets: shares outstanding times share price. For bitcoin, the formula is circulating supply (coins mined so far, about 19.93 million) times the spot price ($114,650 as this page loaded). Two caveats matter. First, "circulating" is generous — an estimated 3 to 4 million BTC are provably lost or dormant since the Satoshi era, so the economically liquid float is smaller than the headline supply. Second, market cap is not money invested: a $2.3 trillion valuation does not mean $2.3 trillion flowed in. Price is set at the margin, by the last trade, and a fraction of the float changing hands can move the entire number.

Bulls frame the comparison as a ladder: if bitcoin matures into a mainstream store of value, the rung above is gold at roughly ten times its current size. Skeptics frame the same table as proof of froth — a zero-cash-flow asset priced above nearly every productive company. Both readings are live; the table simply tells you where the debate is priced.

Total cryptocurrency market cap

Bitcoin is the anchor of a broader crypto market valued near $4 trillion in October 2026, of which BTC alone is about 57% — the "dominance" figure traders watch as a rotation gauge. Rising dominance usually signals risk-off behavior within crypto; falling dominance marks speculative seasons in smaller tokens. Notably, the second and third largest crypto assets (ether and tether) together are worth less than half of bitcoin, which is why this site covers one asset and not ten thousand.
