What moves the BTC/USD price

Bitcoin's price is set the old-fashioned way: bids meeting offers on open order books, around the clock, on every inhabited continent. There is no opening bell and no closing auction. When you see a single "bitcoin price," you are looking at an index — a weighted blend of quotes from venues like Coinbase, Kraken and Bitstamp — because no two exchanges clear at exactly the same number.

In 2026 the dominant price drivers look different from earlier cycles. Spot ETF flows — the daily creation and redemption of shares in funds like BlackRock's IBIT — now absorb or supply thousands of BTC per day and get published every afternoon. The April 2024 halving cut new issuance to 3.125 BTC per block, roughly 450 coins a day, which means ETF demand alone regularly exceeds fresh supply by an order of magnitude. And US macro policy still sets the tide: bitcoin has traded like a high-beta liquidity asset since 2020, rallying when real yields fall and de-risking when the dollar strengthens.

How to read the number on this page

The quote above is a spot reference, not a guaranteed execution price. If you place a market order for a meaningful size, you will walk the order book and pay slightly more (or receive slightly less) than the mid-quote — the difference is slippage, and on liquid pairs it is usually a few basis points. The 24-hour change is a rolling window, not a calendar day, so it can look "wrong" against a candlestick chart that opens at midnight UTC.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current Bitcoin price in USD?

The live quote at the top of this page refreshes every 60 seconds. As of October 2026, bitcoin has spent most of the month trading between $110,000 and $115,000.

Why does the BTC price differ between exchanges?

Each exchange runs its own order book. Spreads between major USD venues are typically under 0.1%; this page shows a blended index rather than any single venue's last trade.

Does Bitcoin ever close?

No. The network has run continuously since January 3, 2009, and spot markets price it every second of every day, weekends and holidays included.
